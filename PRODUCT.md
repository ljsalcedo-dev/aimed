# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Medical students studying for USMLE, NCLEX, and other licensing exams. Using aimed alone, usually in focused study sessions — reviewing concepts they're shaky on, drilling flashcards before an exam, or simulating clinical reasoning. Potentially shared with classmates, so the interface should be self-explanatory to someone who has never seen it before. These users are often cognitively loaded; anything that adds friction or demands attention from the UI itself is harmful.

## Product Purpose

aimed is a local AI study tool with three study modes: a streaming Q&A chat for concept questions, an SM-2 spaced-repetition flashcard library with AI card generation, and a clinical case simulator where MedGemma plays attending. All data stays in localStorage; the model runs locally via Ollama. Success looks like a student finishing a study session feeling more prepared and less anxious — not impressed by the app, but sharpened by it.

## Brand Personality

Calm, supportive, clear. The app is a steady study partner, not a product trying to sell itself. It answers confidently, holds structure without rigidity, and never makes the student feel judged for not knowing something.

## Anti-references

- **Generic med-app (white + teal)**: WebMD, Medscape, consumer health portals — clinically sterile, teal/green accent color, feels like a hospital waiting room.
- **SaaS dashboard clichés**: Hero metric cards, gradient text, identical icon-grid layouts, anything that looks AI-generated or built from a template.
- **Gamified learning apps**: Duolingo-style streaks, stars, badges, bright primary colors, playful illustrations — aims for engagement over effectiveness.
- **Startup landing page energy**: Big hero sections, testimonials, pricing — anything that looks like it is trying to acquire users rather than serve them.

## Design Principles

1. **Study without friction** — Every interaction should reduce cognitive overhead. Navigation, feedback, and controls should require zero attention from the student.
2. **Earned confidence** — The interface should make students feel like they are getting sharper, not entertained. Information hierarchy matters more than visual embellishment.
3. **Quiet authority** — AI-powered features should feel capable without announcing themselves. No badges, no "AI" labels on every surface.
4. **Contained and private** — The tool runs locally; the design should feel self-contained and trustworthy, not cloud-connected or data-hungry.
5. **Depth over decoration** — Neutral surfaces let the content lead. Color, when used, carries meaning — it is not applied for visual interest.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA as a baseline. Dark mode supported. Reduced-motion preference should be respected for any animation added. No specific known user accessibility needs beyond that.
